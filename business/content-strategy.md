# Nokta — Content Strategy

## Content Philosophy

Content at Nokta serves three purposes:

1. **Educate** — Help developers understand the context problem and how Nokta solves it
2. **Build trust** — Demonstrate expertise, honesty, and genuine desire to help
3. **Drive discovery** — Make Nokta findable when developers search for solutions

Content should feel like a senior engineer explaining something genuinely interesting, not a marketing team trying to rank for keywords.

---

## Content Pillars

### Pillar 1: The Context Problem

**What it covers:** Why AI tools lose project context, how it compounds over time, the invisible cost
**Tone:** Educational, slightly provocative, grounded in developer experience
**Goal:** Developers recognize their own pain and see Nokta as the solution

**Content types:**

- Long-form essays (1,500-3,000 words): "The Invisible Cost of Context Loss in AI-Assisted Development"
- Twitter threads: Pain-point specific provocations
- Short posts: Quick observations about AI tool behavior

**Sample topics:**

- "Your AI assistant doesn't remember your project" (context loss)
- "Why every AI session starts from scratch" (cold start problem)
- "The difference between knowing a codebase and knowing project context" (context vs code)
- "Context debt is invisible until it compounds" (accumulation problem)

### Pillar 2: Technical Deep Dives

**What it covers:** How Nokta works under the hood, architecture decisions, implementation details
**Tone:** Technical, detailed, honest about tradeoffs
**Goal:** Developers trust Nokta because they understand how it works

**Content types:**

- Architecture posts (2,000-4,000 words): Detailed explanation of specific systems
- Tutorial posts (1,000-2,000 words): How to use specific features effectively
- Decision records: Why Nokta made specific architectural choices

**Sample topics:**

- "How Nokta's context compiler works" (technical deep dive)
- "The `.ai/trail/` protocol spec" (open standard documentation)
- "Designing the quality gate system" (architecture decision)
- "Token optimization: how we achieve 70% context reduction"
- "Pattern extraction: from codebase to compiled context"

### Pillar 3: Use Case Guides

**What it covers:** How to use Nokta for specific scenarios
**Tone:** Practical, step-by-step, result-focused
**Goal:** Developers can implement specific workflows quickly

**Content types:**

- How-to guides (800-1,500 words): Complete walkthroughs of specific use cases
- Video demos: 60-90 second demonstrations of key features
- Integration guides: How to connect Nokta with specific tools

**Sample topics:**

- "Setting up Nokta for a React + Node.js project" (stack-specific guide)
- "How to track architectural decisions in Nokta" (decision engine tutorial)
- "Using quality gates for security scanning" (advanced feature guide)
- "Autonomous agents: practical overnight workflows" (agent tutorial)
- "Nokta + Cursor: a complete workflow guide" (integration guide)

### Pillar 4: Team & Enterprise

**What it covers:** How Nokta works for teams, compliance, enterprise deployment
**Tone:** Professional, solution-oriented, compliance-aware
**Goal:** Enterprise buyers feel confident Nokta meets their requirements

**Content types:**

- Case studies (1,000-2,000 words): Real team results with specific metrics
- Comparison posts (1,500-2,500 words): Nokta vs alternatives, with honest assessments
- Compliance documentation: Security whitepaper, audit guide, data handling docs

**Sample topics:**

- "How to evaluate AI infrastructure for compliance" (buying guide)
- "Nokta for engineering teams: a case study" (team case study)
- "Security architecture: how Nokta handles sensitive data" (security whitepaper)
- "Enterprise deployment: self-hosted vs cloud" (deployment comparison)

### Pillar 5: Industry Perspective

**What it covers:** Nokta's view on the broader AI developer tool landscape
**Tone:** Opinionated, forward-looking, honest
**Goal:** Position Nokta as a thought leader, not just a product

**Content types:**

- Opinion posts (800-1,500 words): Nokta's take on industry trends
- Predictions: Where AI developer tools are heading
- Reactions: Thoughtful response to major industry announcements

**Sample topics:**

- "The AI coding tool fragmentation problem" (industry observation)
- "Why local-first AI infrastructure is the future" (trend prediction)
- "What happens when every developer uses AI?" (broader impact)
- "The difference between AI tools and AI infrastructure" (market positioning)

## Content Quality Standards

### What Good Nokta Content Looks Like

**Does:**

- Address a specific, real developer pain point
- Provide actionable guidance (not just concepts)
- Shows real examples from real projects
- Acknowledges limitations and tradeoffs honestly
- Uses appropriate technical depth for the audience
- Includes clear next steps or calls to action

**Doesn't:**

- Oversell or make unrealistic claims
- Use buzzwords without substance
- Repeat generic AI tool marketing language
- Bury the lede in corporate language
- Ignore opposing viewpoints

### Voice Guidelines

**Write like:**

- A senior engineer explaining something to a junior
- Someone who's actually used the tools they're discussing
- Someone who cares more about solving problems than winning arguments

**Don't write like:**

- A marketing team trying to rank for keywords
- A vendor selling something
- A hype person chasing the latest trend

## Assumptions

1. Developers trust content that demonstrates deep technical understanding
2. Honest acknowledgment of limitations builds more trust than overselling
3. Tutorial content drives more initial adoption than philosophical content
4. Case studies with specific numbers are more persuasive than general claims
5. SEO value compounds over time — consistency matters more than perfection
6. Twitter/X is the highest-leverage platform for developer-focused content
